    Jo Banner

    Founder and Director, The Descendants Project

    Read bio of Jo Banner

    Jo Banner is the founder and director of The Descendants Project, a nonprofit she co-founded with her twin sister, Dr. Joy Banner, in St. John the Baptist Parish, Louisiana. Holding bachelor’s and master’s degrees in communications, she uses her education to honor the legacy of enslaved people while defending descendant communities.

    Living in Louisiana’s Cancer Alley, Jo is a fierce advocate for environmental justice, working to eliminate pollution from grain to petrochemicals. She represents frontline communities in global spaces like the UN Plastics Pollution Treaty, highlighting the health toll of plastic production on Black communities and other fenceline residents.

    Jo’s mission includes preserving the burial grounds of the enslaved and protecting the last 11 miles untouched by industrial development in Cancer Alley. Through her work, she envisions a just transition for people and land to achieve economic and environmental liberation.

  8. Speaker

    Emin Bayramli

    Son of Dr. Gubad Ibadoghlu

    Read bio of Emin Bayramli

    Emin Bayramli is the son of Dr. Gubad Ibadoghlu, a renowned economist, anti-corruption scholar, and human rights defender who was arrested in Azerbaijan in July 2023 following years of research and advocacy focused on corruption and transparency in Azerbaijan’s oil and gas sector.

    Emin has lived in the United States for the past 10 years and is based between New Jersey and Washington, D.C. Since his father’s arrest, he has been at the forefront of advocacy efforts to secure his freedom, engaging with members of Congress from both sides of the aisle, human rights organizations, academics, and other advocates to bring attention to his father’s case.

    April 18, 2026 marked 1,000 days since his family began their fight for Dr. Ibadoghlu’s freedom. Emin continues to seek support and collaboration to raise awareness about his father’s case and the broader issues surrounding human rights and political repression in Azerbaijan.

    Emin is a graduate of Rutgers University, where he earned his degree in Statistics.

2026 Sentinels

The Community Sentinel Award for Environmental Stewardship honors leaders across the U.S. working to protect their communities from environmental injustice.

Eboni Neal Cochran (she/her)

Louisville, KY

Organization
Rubbertown Emergency ACTion (REACT)
Introduced by
Dakota Lewis

Eboni is a wife and retired homeschool mom of one, who has three adult bonus children and six grandchildren.

She is Co-Director of Rubbertown Emergency ACTion (REACT), a grassroots organization of residents living near or at the fence lines of Rubbertown chemical facilities and other industries that cause environmental injustice. She has worked with the organization since 2003, educating community members and decision makers and pushing for reduced exposure to toxic chemicals.

REACT works for strong laws to stop toxic air pollution, protection of residents in a leak, fire or explosion at an industrial facility or railcar, and full disclosure of information on the impact of industrial facilities.

Eboni is also founder and executive director of the West Louisville Math and Science Project, which builds math, science and literacy skills among children within the African diaspora and their families.

Zulene Mayfield (she/her)

Chester, PA

Organization
Chester Residents Concerned for Quality Living (CRCQL)
Introduced by
Hilary Flint

Zulene was born, raised and educated in Chester. She is a family-oriented person who enjoys the comfort of a large and loving family, and it was that love of family that led her to join a group of residents concerned about what was being billed as the Westinghouse trash-to-steam plant. She was the youngest of the group and the only woman of color. She became chairperson of Chester Residents Concerned for Quality Living (CRCQL) in 1993, a year after the group formed.

CRCQL has defeated numerous waste processing facilities, shut down the nation’s largest processor of infectious medical waste, and successfully sued a sewage treatment and sludge burning plant, resulting in increased EPA and DEP fines. It also filed a Title VI lawsuit against the DEP alleging a discriminatory practice of issuing permits in Chester, the first of its kind in the nation, and its actions led Pennsylvania to create the Office of Environmental Justice.

Zulene helped form Campus Coalition Concerning Chester (C4) at Swarthmore College, has served on the National Environmental Justice Advisory Committee (NEJAC), and advises C4 and CRCQL’s Choices Environmental Youth Leadership Group. She is now leading her community and its allies in mobilizing against Penn America LNG, a proposed liquefaction facility that would be the largest LNG facility on the East Coast.

Loice Malvo (she/her)

Lake Charles, LA

Organization
Fisherville Environmental Action Now
Introduced by
Miss Debra Ramirez

At 80 years young, Loice Malvo has spent more than four decades standing up for her community, guided by an unwavering faith in God and a deep commitment to justice. A longtime advocate in Southwest Louisiana, Loice has confronted environmental threats, racism, poverty, and inequity while working to protect the health and dignity of her neighbors. She has spoken before state representatives in Baton Rouge and at the U.S. Capitol, and has shared her community’s concerns with KPLC-TV, American Crest TV, and other media outlets. Loice successfully challenged the rezoning of Rice Mill from residential to industrial and opposed a pipeline near an elementary school. She also fought a proposed duplex on a landfill on St. Mary’s Drive, helping residents move to cleaner, safer housing. Through Fisherville Environmental Action Now (FEAN), founded after toxic chemical train derailments affected her family and community, Loice continues to educate and mobilize residents. Her work reflects her belief in spiritual warfare, compassion, unity, and protecting God’s people and creation.

Emerging Leader Award

Qiyam Ansari (he/him)

Monongahela Valley, PA

Organization
Valley Clean Air Now (VCAN)
Introduced by
NaTisha Washington

Qiyam is the Executive Director of Valley Clean Air Now (VCAN), an environmental justice nonprofit fighting for clean air in Pennsylvania’s Mon Valley, home to U.S. Steel’s Clairton Coke Works. A community organizer rooted in the region, he has worked since 2019 to help residents hold industrial polluters accountable and assert their right to clean air under Pennsylvania’s Environmental Rights Amendment.

With Clean Water Action, he led the campaign that won Pittsburgh’s plastic bag ban, helped secure EPA rule changes that reduced toxic emissions from coke oven batteries through fenceline benzene monitoring, and spearheaded a Community Health Assessment with the Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry.

He has raised more than $4.5 million for grassroots groups in Allegheny County and serves on the Allegheny County Air Pollution Control Advisory Committee.

Community Choice Award

Karen Feridun (she/her)

Kutztown, PA

Organization
Better Path Coalition, No False Climate Solutions PA
Introduced by
Gillian Graber, 2023 Community Sentinel

Karen is a Pennsylvania-based activist and co-founder of two statewide coalitions, the Better Path Coalition and No False Climate Solutions PA. Her work in the anti-fracking movement began in 2010 when she founded Berks Gas Truth, and she went on to co-found Pennsylvanians Against Fracking and serve on the national steering committees of Americans Against Fracking and the Stop the Frack Attack Network. In 2022 and 2023, she was the lead organizer of the Pennsylvania Climate Convergence in the state capital. Her work spans local, regional, national and international levels, and she has written and spoken on these issues in The Huffington Post, Daily Kos and The Hill, and at a COP21 side event, the DNC platform committee meeting in Orlando, the National Geographic Society and New York’s Society for Ethical Culture. In 2020, she was invited by The Philadelphia Inquirer to argue in favor of banning fracking, opposite then–Lt. Gov. John Fetterman, who argued against a ban.

Legacy of Heroes

The Legacy of Heroes honors environmental advocates who have recently passed on. Their photos and descriptions will be displayed throughout the room. Please take time to view them before the awards ceremony begins at 7 PM.

We pay our respects to:

  • Jim and Terry FitzgeraldBayfield, Colorado, 1939–2025 and 1935–2025
    Read about Jim and Terry

    Jim and Terry Fitzgerald were giants in the oil and gas accountability movement that took root in La Plata County, Colorado, in the late 1980s, when the county became the worldwide guinea pig for the industry’s massive experiment in coalbed methane development. They fought for landowners to have a say in what happened on their own land, helped found the Oil & Gas Accountability Project (now Earthworks) in 1999, and organized People’s Oil & Gas Summits in communities facing drilling booms. Jim and Terry were the very essence of participatory democracy—not the abstract kind, but the real, gritty, show-up, do-the-work kind. They didn’t wait for permission or for someone else to lead. They showed us that change comes from ordinary people who care deeply, act boldly, and refuse to look away.

  • Mary Ann AtencioTorreon (Na'neelzhiin), Navajo Nation, 1954–2025
    Read about Mary Ann

    Mary Ann Atencio, a respected clan matriarch of the Torreon community on the Navajo Nation, was a plaintiff in the landmark Atencio et al. v. State of New Mexico state constitutional lawsuit. She was unhesitant to take action to protect the land, air, water, and sacred places for future generations of her Navajo People.

  • Julie Weatherington-RiceColumbus, Ohio, 1948–2025
    Read about Julie

    Dr. Julie Weatherington-Rice was a fierce defender of the environment and a dedicated educator who believed in the power of knowledge to create change. Through her scientific research, teaching, and tireless advocacy for clean water, she left a lasting legacy of protecting both the natural world and the people who depend upon it.

  • Tami MewisPurmela, Texas, 1960–2025
    Read about Tami

    Tami Mewis was a painter in Austin who built a small trailer park community on land in the Texas countryside. When the DeLa Express Pipeline was routed through her property, she never missed a Hold the Line meeting, fighting to protect the community she had made.

  • Kate BartholomewMontour Falls, New York, 1963–2026
    Read about Kate

    Kate Bartholomew was a fearless warrior for justice, especially when it came to protecting the natural world. For two decades she was a volunteer leader with the Sierra Club, and for the past eight years chair of its Atlantic Chapter. She played a major leadership role in achieving New York’s fracking ban, and as a science teacher at Watkins Glen High School and the New Roots Charter School she left behind a large “pack” of students whose lives she touched. As she put it, “And now we face the most monumental, existential crisis humanity has ever seen—most especially because of our part in creating it—so the moral demand to engage in this struggle becomes even more imperative—for the planet, for the future, for all life.”

  • Delma “Bubee” BennettMossville, Louisiana, 1944–2026
    Read about Delma

    Delma Leroy “Bubee” Bennett was a Mossville resident and activist and treasurer of Mossville Environmental Action Now. With his wife of 53 years, he spoke out about the pollution harming their neighbors. “It’s just a handful of people like my wife and myself who continue to try to make things better for themselves,” he said. An Army drill sergeant and Head Elder at Sharon Chapel Seventh-day Adventist Church, he is remembered for his faith, his laughter, and his gift for bringing people together.

  • Jim BruggersLouisville, Kentucky, 1958–2026
    Read about Jim

    Jim Bruggers’ decades of dogged reporting shined a light on polluting corporations, inadequate regulations and the people who fought against them for environmental justice. At the Louisville Courier Journal he became known as a watchdog of Louisville’s air, water and land, and at Inside Climate News he covered coal, petrochemicals and plastics across the Southeast. When he wanted to cover something in depth, he would rent a car and start knocking on doors.

  • Marcia CarnahanSlippery Rock, Pennsylvania, 1945–2025
    Read about Marcia

    When a cold-mix asphalt plant was proposed directly across the street from her home, Marcia Carnahan knew she had to take action. She co-founded Save Slippery Rock Creek, canvassed door to door, and set up tables at local events, uniting her community to protect the creek. She truly embodied the spirit of local activism and care for the environment.

  • Melissa Bright-BennettConvent, Louisiana, 1978–2026
